# Indicators

Make explicit what observable developments would warrant reconsidering an assessment.

Use the shortest complete response that preserves the assessment's scope,
uncertainty, and evidence status. For a clear ordinary-language invocation that
does not name INDICATORS, begin with one line:
`INDICATORS — <brief context-specific description>.` Omit the cue for an
explicit command or mere discussion. Conditional signals are not observations,
and their absence matters only when detection was reasonably expected.

## Operation

- Use context to identify the assessment or competing explanations and their uncertainty. If it is materially unclear what is being assessed, ask one brief question.
- Select developments that could strengthen, weaken, or change the assessment. Connect each to the particular judgment it bears on and explain why it matters, including which explanations it would or would not distinguish.
- Define what could be observed, rather than restating the assessment in different words. Separate an intermediate mechanism working from the ultimate outcome improving; neither alone necessarily establishes causation.
- Specify timing, thresholds, or reassessment triggers when the supplied evidence or context justifies them. Otherwise use qualitative conditions and identify what would be needed to calibrate a threshold; do not invent precision or probabilities.
- Before treating an absent signal as informative, consider whether it should have appeared by then and whether observation would reliably detect it. Silence from a weak reporting channel is not evidence of no problem.
- Keep the set proportional to the question, avoiding redundant signals and acknowledging when several observations may share the same source or cause.

## Boundaries

- Indicators are conditional expectations, not findings that the developments have occurred or proof of a favored explanation.
- Do not invent observation channels or imply access to private records. Suggested measurements remain proposals.
- Work in the conversational agent using available context; no worker or external service is required.
- Do not automatically research, verify, create monitoring, schedule tasks, save records, or promise future alerts. Defining what to watch does not authorize watching it.
