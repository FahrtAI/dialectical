# Analysis of Competing Hypotheses

Use ACH to identify the explanation least contradicted by the available evidence. It does not prove a hypothesis true.

## Method

1. State the decision or observation being explained without embedding a favored answer.
2. Define genuinely competing hypotheses. Make overlaps explicit; do not create cosmetic variants to fill a matrix.
3. List material evidence, including absences, assumptions, source limitations, and observations that would normally be expected but are missing.
4. Check whether evidence items share the same underlying source or mechanism. Do not count correlated repetitions as independent support.
5. For each evidence–hypothesis pair, assess:
   - `++` strongly consistent
   - `+` consistent
   - `0` neutral or non-diagnostic
   - `-` inconsistent
   - `--` strongly inconsistent
   - `?` insufficient information
6. Weight diagnostic evidence more heavily than evidence compatible with every hypothesis. Focus on contradictions rather than counting supporting marks.
7. Seek plausible evidence that would disconfirm the leading hypothesis. Reassess after adding it.
8. Run a sensitivity check: identify which uncertain evidence or weighting could change the ranking.
9. Select the least-inconsistent hypothesis and report residual uncertainty, rejected alternatives, and the next most discriminating test.

## Output

Include:

- the hypotheses;
- the evidence matrix;
- source-dependence or diagnosticity notes;
- the least-inconsistent assessment;
- sensitivity and residual uncertainty;
- the next evidence that would most change the result.

Do not total symbols mechanically. One reliable contradiction may matter more than many weak consistencies.
