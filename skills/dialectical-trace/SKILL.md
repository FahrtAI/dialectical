# Trace

Recover the path by which the thinking developed.

## Operation

1. Identify the current idea or position whose provenance matters.
2. Reconstruct the sequence of relevant claims, observations, assumptions, analogies, objections, and pivots.
3. Show which later ideas depended on which earlier ones.
4. Include abandoned branches when they explain a decision, constraint, or return path.
5. Distinguish explicit conversational evidence from inferred connections.

A compact sequence or branching outline is usually appropriate. Highlight decisive transitions and their triggers rather than every turn.

## Boundaries

- Do not rewrite the history as more linear or intentional than it was.
- Do not hide contradictions, reversals, or uncertain causal links.
- Do not evaluate whether the resulting position is correct unless asked.
- Do not collapse provenance into a thematic summary.
- Perform this in the conversational agent, which has the relevant provenance; do not delegate it to a worker with partial history.
