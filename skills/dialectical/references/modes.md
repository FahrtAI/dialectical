# Kinds of conversation

Serious conversations usually do one of four kinds of work. Each is legitimate for its purpose. Trouble comes from using one kind for another's purpose, or from drifting between them without noticing.

| | Debate | Narrative | Inquiry | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Question** | Which position wins? | What story explains this? | What might be true, and how could we tell? | Given what we know and value, what should we do? |
| **Produces** | A defended position | An explanatory story | Testable hypotheses, evidence, and calibrated beliefs | A decision, policy, or strategy |
| **Typical failure** | Binary thinking; evidence used as ammunition | Coherence mistaken for truth | Testing without end, or stopping at a list of possibilities without saying which survived | Goals, values, or preferences passed off as facts |
| **Sounds like** | "Make the case for…", "Who's right?", "Rebut this" | "How did we get here?", "What's the story?", "Explain why" | "Why might…?", "How would we know?", "Is it true that…?" | "Should we…?", "Which option?", "What's the plan?" |
| **Operations that fit** | RESTATE each side; CHALLENGE each position; SOURCE-QUALITY on cited evidence | TRACE; HYPOTHESIZE rival stories; CAUSAL-CHAIN on the story's links | HYPOTHESIZE, THOUGHT EXPERIMENT, ACH, TEST DESIGN, RESEARCH, VERIFY, CHALLENGE | DISTILL the factual basis; CHALLENGE the plan; TEST DESIGN unresolved empirical assumptions; CAUSAL-CHAIN on expected effects |

## When each one is right

- **Debate** fits when the user needs to make or stress-test a case: preparing for a negotiation, drafting an argument, steelmanning an opponent. Do not force inquiry onto a request for the strongest case, but do not present the case as the balance of the evidence.
- **Narrative** fits when the user needs an explanation others can follow: a postmortem, a presentation, onboarding material. Keep track of which links in the story are established and which fill gaps.
- **Inquiry** fits when the aim is to find out what is true. Prefer it whenever that is the aim, even if the conversation started as a debate or a story.
- **Decision** fits when action is required. Evidence rarely settles a decision alone; it also depends on goals, values, constraints, and trade-offs.

## Recognizing the kind

RELEVANCE can clarify purpose in any of these conversations without converting
exploration into a decision. COLLECTION GAPS can identify evidence that would
change understanding or distinguish explanations. TEST DESIGN can turn a
selected claim or gap into an executable test. INDICATORS can define what
developments would warrant reassessment. Use these only when requested; none is
a stage, and their outputs do not automatically launch another operation.

Infer the kind from the question and from what the user plans to do with the answer. Do not ask the user to classify the conversation. Ask only when the difference would change the response materially, for example: "Do you want the strongest case for this, or to find out whether it's true?"

Do not announce or label the kind of conversation. Show it through the work: separate facts from values in a decision, mark which parts of a story are established, and keep a debate's case distinct from the evidence.

## Common drifts

| Drift | Sign | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry → narrative | Each turn adds detail to the same explanation, and no new evidence arrives | Offer the check described under Monitor |
| Inquiry → debate | An open question is reduced to two sides | Ask what other possibilities exist, or offer HYPOTHESIZE |
| Inquiry → decision | "So we should…" | Separate what is established, what is assumed, and what is a choice about goals or values |
| Decision → debate | Options turn into camps | Restate the criteria and compare the options against them |
| Decision passed off as fact | A value judgment phrased as a finding ("the right price is X") | Name the goal or value the claim depends on |
