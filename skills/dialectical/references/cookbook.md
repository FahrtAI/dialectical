# In-chat cookbook

Use this reference when the user asks for the cookbook, a practical example, or
how Dialectical could help with a particular situation. Show the closest recipe
or, when the user asks for the whole cookbook, show all recipes in a readable
form. These are examples, not workflows. The user can skip, reorder, branch, or
stop at any point.

Showing a recipe does not invoke any operation. Do not conduct research, verify
a claim, save material, monitor indicators, or apply another move until the user
separately asks for it.

## Check something before you share it

**Opening:** “This market estimate is going into the board deck tomorrow.”

CHALLENGE the claim before it leaves the conversation: what it assumes, where
the number came from, and what would make it wrong. The challenge ends with what
holds up, so you know what you can say with confidence and what to soften or
source. VERIFY can check a specific figure when evidence is available.

**Minimal move:** CHALLENGE.

## Explore a new idea

**Opening:** “Could small neighborhood rituals make a place feel more like
home?”

Talk normally about what “ritual” and “belonging” mean. RELEVANCE can clarify
the inquiry when scope matters. RESEARCH can look for prior work, including
cases where the proposed mechanism failed. Resemblance to an existing theory is
not validation.

**Minimal move:** RELEVANCE.

## Explain an unexpected outcome

**Opening:** “Churn increased after our redesign. What might explain it?”

Separate the observed change from its interpretation. HYPOTHESIZE can generate
genuinely competing explanations. ACH can compare them once evidence exists.
COLLECTION GAPS can identify evidence that would distinguish them without
collecting it. TEST DESIGN can specify how a selected gap would be measured and
adjudicated. Timing alone does not establish causation.

**Minimal move:** HYPOTHESIZE.

## Test a causal claim

**Opening:** “Restoring export will bring churn back to 3%. Does that follow?”

CAUSAL-CHAIN can inspect each link and expose the assumptions carrying the
prediction. VERIFY can later check one specific empirical link if requested.
An unsupported link is uncertain, not automatically false.

**Minimal move:** CAUSAL-CHAIN.

## Turn critique into an actionable test

**Opening:** “We have three hypotheses and several ways each could fail. What
should we test first?”

TEST DESIGN can convert the claims and vulnerabilities into minimum
discriminating tests with measurements, controls, decision rules, dependencies,
and consequences. It can use prior CHALLENGE, ACH, or COLLECTION GAPS work, but
does not require a fixed sequence. A test plan is generated methodology, not a
finding, and the operation does not conduct the proposed research.

**Minimal move:** TEST DESIGN.

## Make a decision under uncertainty

**Opening:** “Should we renew our office lease?”

Start with the options, values, constraints, and time horizon. RELEVANCE can
clarify what the decision needs to serve. COLLECTION GAPS can prioritize
evidence worth seeking now. INDICATORS can define developments that would
justify reconsideration later. Neither operation researches or monitors by
itself, and evidence does not determine values.

**Minimal move:** RELEVANCE.

## Evaluate a source or claim

**Opening:** “This vendor says its chatbot cut tickets in half. How much weight
should I give that?”

SOURCE-QUALITY can assess the supplied source and evidence. VERIFY can check a
precise empirical proposition against external evidence when requested. A
report's existence does not prove its contents, and verification remains
incomplete when the required evidence is unavailable.

**Minimal move:** SOURCE-QUALITY for a supplied report; VERIFY for a factual
check.

## Develop a strategy

**Opening:** “We want to grow without overwhelming support.”

Explore constraints and plausible options first. THOUGHT EXPERIMENT can expose
conditional implications. CHALLENGE can stress-test a developed option.
INDICATORS can define signs that would justify revision. A hypothetical result
is not empirical evidence.

**Minimal move:** CHALLENGE when a strategy already exists.

## Compare competing explanations

**Opening:** “We have three explanations and six observations. Which survives?”

Agree on the observations and identify shared source origins. ACH can compare
the explanations while preserving unknowns. VERIFY can check a disputed item if
separately requested. The least-inconsistent explanation is not thereby proven.

**Minimal move:** ACH.

## Work through disagreement

**Opening:** “We keep disagreeing about whether remote work hurts mentoring.”

Clarify what each person claims. RESTATE can preserve the positions and their
qualifiers. RELEVANCE can reveal that the parties are answering different
questions or applying different values. CHALLENGE can test a selected argument.
Agreement and rhetorical strength are not evidence.

**Minimal move:** RESTATE.

## Preserve a long inquiry

**Opening:** “I'm losing track of what we learned.”

RECAP can compress what was discussed. DISTILL can separate findings, generated
ideas, and uncertainty. TRACE can reconstruct important pivots. SAVE can export the relevant conversation as
reusable Markdown for saving or importing into a new thread. None of these
claims cross-thread memory or persistence that did not occur.

**Minimal move:** RECAP for coverage; DISTILL for takeaways; SAVE for a portable
handoff.

After showing a recipe, the conversation can continue normally. If the user
wants help choosing among its moves, GUIDE can recommend options when explicitly
requested; displaying the recipe itself does not run GUIDE.
