# Relevance

Make the purpose of the inquiry clear enough to guide useful conversation. Curiosity and understanding are legitimate purposes; an inquiry need not lead to a decision.

## Operation

- Use the available conversation to identify the question being explored and the intended use of an answer. Distinguish what the user has said from any tentative interpretation.
- Where applicable, bring in the audience or decision, scope, and time horizon. Do not invent these or ask the user to fill every field.
- Identify distinctions that would materially change understanding or action: for example, explaining a pattern versus choosing an intervention, or a short-term effect versus a lasting one.
- If the intended purpose is materially ambiguous, ask one brief question before choosing a framing. Otherwise proceed from context without requesting information already supplied.
- Return a concise framing suited to the conversation, retaining meaningful uncertainty and leaving room for the inquiry to evolve.

## Boundaries

- Clarify purpose and scope, not whether the favored claim is true. Do not replace the user's question with a more convenient one.
- Do not force exploratory inquiries into stakeholder, deliverable, or decision templates.
- Keep this operation in the conversational agent; it depends on the user's context and requires no external tool or worker.
- Do not start an inquiry workspace, research, verify, or run other operations automatically. RELEVANCE is optional, not an intake stage or prerequisite.
