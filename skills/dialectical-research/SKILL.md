# Research

Expand what is known around the current object of thought without prematurely collapsing the inquiry to one answer.

Use the shortest complete response that preserves material source provenance,
independence, scope, disagreement, and uncertainty; expand when the evidence
requires it. For a clear ordinary-language invocation that does not name
RESEARCH, begin with one line: `RESEARCH — <brief context-specific description>.`
Omit the cue for an explicit command, mere discussion, or internal evidence
work. Change confidence only when the retrieved evidence justifies it.

## Operation

1. State or infer the research focus and the decisions or questions it should inform.
2. Search available external sources. Prefer primary, authoritative, and recent sources where the subject requires them.
3. Include relevant prior art, competing schools of thought, analogous cases, and negative or limiting evidence—not only material that fits the current framing.
4. Separate established findings, informed interpretation, and speculation.
5. Synthesize the evidence around the inquiry and identify useful gaps or follow-up questions.
6. Cite sources close to the claims they support and note consequential source limitations.

Let the subject determine the structure. Optimize for expansion and orientation, not a predetermined number of sources.

## Ideas the conversation produced

When the focus is a mechanism, distinction, explanation, or intervention that emerged in the conversation, look for prior work before elaborating it:

- Search by structure, not wording. Ask who has studied something structurally equivalent, what they called it, and where else it has appeared; the relevant work may use entirely different terms.
- Look for competing theories, contradicting evidence, failed replications, boundary conditions, and known failures, not only matching work.
- Report four parts: the conversation's own formulation; prior work; empirical support that actually tests the mechanism; and the novel remainder, if any, after comparison.

Prior work is not validation. Finding an established concept that resembles the idea starts the investigation; it does not confirm the idea or make it more authoritative.

## Delegation

When the host supports isolated workers and the research separates into substantive independent workstreams, delegate by question, evidence class, or competing view. Give each worker the shared research focus, decision context, scope, and source requirements; do not send unrelated conversation history. The conversational agent checks source overlap, reconciles contradictions, and owns the synthesis. Handle a short search or an ordered investigation directly rather than spawning workers for source count alone.

## Boundaries

- Do not present remembered facts as researched evidence when external sources were unavailable.
- Do not cherry-pick support for the current explanation.
- Do not silently change an exploratory request into a verdict on one proposition; that is VERIFY.
- Do not imply that source quantity compensates for weak source quality.
- Do not treat a resemblance to established work as evidence that an idea is true, or a failed search for the conversation's own wording as evidence that it is new.
